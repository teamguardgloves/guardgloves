Friction Wear Performance

Abrasion Resistance Ratings

Abrasion resistance evaluates a glove's ability to withstand surface wear caused by repeated friction and contact with abrasive materials. During testing, glove material is rubbed against a standardized abrasive surface until it wears through.

ANSI/ISEA 105 assigns abrasion resistance levels from 0 to 6, with Level 6 providing the highest abrasion resistance.

EN 388 assigns abrasion resistance levels from 1 to 4, with Level 4 offering the highest level of abrasion protection.

Higher abrasion ratings indicate greater durability and are recommended for demanding applications where gloves are exposed to continuous wear.

Industries including construction, metal fabrication, manufacturing, mining, oil & gas, automotive, and material handling rely on abrasion-resistant gloves to improve safety, extend glove life, and reduce replacement costs.

Regulatory Framework Changes

Recent Updates to ANSI/ISEA 105

The November 2024 update to ANSI/ISEA 105 standards aims to improve clarity and present a glove's safety features in an easily understandable way. The new standard introduces a pentagon-shaped pictogram on each glove, displaying the glove's cut, abrasion, and puncture resistance ratings.

The number on the left side of the pentagon indicates the glove's abrasion resistance. Abrasion resistance levels range from 0 to 6, with 6 providing the highest level of protection. High abrasion resistance is crucial for tasks involving rough or abrasive materials, maintaining the glove's durability over a longer period of time.

High abrasion-resistant gloves provide more than just protection against wear—they also offer greater durability, longer service life, and lower replacement costs. Gloves with higher abrasion resistance maintain their performance even in demanding work environments, making them a cost-effective choice for businesses.
